Get ready to push boundaries

On this BA in 3D Animation course, you will develop creative techniques through a range of digital mediums including film, television, games, and online platforms.

Why study this degree? Why study this degree?

Produce an assessed portfolio of work and get ready to enter the professional world of animation with confidence and know-how.

Gain experience with professional studio software

Explore the software tools used in professional studios

Acquire top-tier industry guidance

Gain opportunities for mentorships with top industry players

Explore live industry briefs

Work on live briefs set by industry professionals

Enhance your technical skills in 3D animation

Apply your creative talent to a successful career in character animation

Be part of a community

Collaborate with other students on creative projects and bring ideas to life

The specifics

  • Animation fundamentals
  • Rigging
  • Acting performance
  • Visual narrative
  • Body mechanics
  • Animation industry
  • Observational and contextual subject related areas

If you are studying the BA (Hons) 3D Animation with Integrated Foundation Year version of this course, key study topics in your foundation year will include:

  • Introduction to the fundamental principles of games and animation
  • Core elements of design and art: line, shape, color, value, form, texture, and space
  • Exploring digital software and applications Relevant to design and user interaction
  • Presenting Research and Ideation with Clarity, Critical Thinking, and Reflection
  • Overview of Digital Games and Animation Software, Including Current Innovations and Limitations of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

If you choose to add an Integrated Foundation Year on this course, you will be first introduced to the fundamental principles of animation and illustration. Your foundation year gives you the time, space and support to explore your strengths, build confidence and prepare for the demands of project-based, industry-focused learning. Modules are as follows:

  • Module 1: Diagnostic exploration - making informed choices
  • Module 2: Technical - Technical knowledge in practice
  • Module 3: Specialist - Innovating specialist practice
  • Module 4: Integration - Integrated practice for progression to Higher Education

Please note: Students starting this course with the Integrated Foundation Year in September 2026/27 will study the 2027/28 approved modules in Years 1, 2 and 3.

Year 1

Master core 3D animation skills, including asset creation, world-building, timing and movement. Gain industry insights through guest lectures, direct creative feedback and collaborative projects

Modules may include: 

  • Rave Beginnings
  • 3D Animation Fundamentals
  • 3D Toolkit - Assets
  • 3D Animation Mechanics & EPC
  • 3D Toolkit - 3D Worlds

Year 2

Elevate your craft with acting, dialogue and narrative storytelling workshops. Gain practical experience through live briefs, industry masterclasses, cross-course collaboration and mentor-led projects.

Modules may include: 

  • Character Development
  • Animation Performance
  • The Storyteller
  • Preparing for Animation & Character Industry
  • Live & Work-based Learning

Year 3

Refine your creative identity, build a professional showreel and develop freelance skills. Blend theory and practice by collaborating with our Games Design and Sound departments to prepare for entering the global animation industry.

Modules may include: 

  • Research & Development
  • Pre-Production
  • Portfolio
  • Rave Futures
  • Major Project Production

A variety of learning and teaching methods are employed in this course. 

These include but are not limited to;

Briefings & lectures (25%), seminars and practical workshops (30%), studio-based project work, demonstrations, collaborative activities including guest lectures, studio visits and industry-informed briefs (25%), group discussions, individual and group tutorials, including critiques, presentations (20%) and self-directed independent study.

The course is assessed entirely by coursework.

Our campus 

You'll study in a building designed to encourage collaboration between the different disciplines and practices within Ravensbourne, and to stimulate the work of innovative professionals and creative collaboration.

Ravensbourne is ideally connected - North Greenwich station is just a 2-minute walk away from our campus and offers amazing views from the Greenwich Peninsula.

These transport links offer an easy route to some of London's major business hubs, perfect for reaching possible placements or networking events with employers in the worlds of business or technology.  

Entry requirements

At Ravensbourne we accept a wide range of qualifications for entry onto our courses, whilst also considering the context in which they were achieved. For this course, we would usually require a GCSE Grade 4 or above in English in addition to any of the below;

  • Three A Levels at grade BBC or above
  • BTEC Extended Diploma at grade DMM
  • UAL Level 3 Extended Diploma
  • T Level (pass or above)
  • Access to Higher Education Diploma
  • International Baccalaureate at grade 25 or above
  • Other/Mixed qualifications equivalent to 112 UCAS Tariff points

At Ravensbourne we take a flexible approach to admissions, as we want you to know exactly what you need to achieve in your exams to guarantee a place on the course. We still think exams are important and look forward to hearing about your successes, but we place just as much importance on the work you will share with us as part of our portfolio process.

Portfolio and interview requirements

Students should ideally have a background in art, creative media or design. Admission onto this course is also subject to successful completion of a portfolio (or showreel). There is no interview requirement for this course.

If you wish to study our BA (Hons) 3D Animation degree, we will need to see examples of previous art, new media and/or design work that showcases your suitability for the course. 

Please provide a digital portfolio of no more than 10 pieces of work.

A piece of work can be: 

  • An animation
  • A storyboard
  • A concept piece, which could be of an environment or character design
  • Life drawings (one sheet of paper with multiple drawings on it or one developed drawing showing tone or colour)
  • Any personal work representing design, drawing, colour and research.
  • 3D work (a single model, an environment or samples of texturing and lighting skills)
  • If you feel other work is suitable such as photography or model making then please feel free to add it if it is relevant to animation.
