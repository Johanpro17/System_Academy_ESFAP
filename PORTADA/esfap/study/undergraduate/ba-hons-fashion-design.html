More reasons to choose this course

You’ll study a blend of traditional and innovative practices, exploring fashion and textile design, pattern cutting, sampling and digital design, discovering how emerging technologies are reshaping the creative process from concept through to production. 

Live briefs and competitions with major international brands have produced winners and finalists with Louis Vuitton, Givenchy, Pierre Cardin, Harris Tweed, Zandra Rhodes, YKK, Calvin Klein and Golden Shears Tailoring. Our association with the British Fashion Council has provided access to materials through sustainability initiatives including donations from Burberry and JW Anderson. Final year students secured prestigious places in the finals of the BFC Sustainable Fabric Initiative for four years running.  

The Fashion department at Ravensbourne also actively engages with London’s creative landscape through gallery and museum visits, bespoke brand and supplier tours, and high-profile collaborations. Sponsored garments from the 2024 Denim Collaboration and Harris Tweed projects were displayed in the Raphael galleries at the V&A as part of the 2025 V&A Fashion Upstart event. 

Graduates from this apparel design course have recently gone on to use their expertise to build careers with high-end companies, as freelancers, or by launching their own independent brands.

The specifics

Students of Fashion Design and Fashion Design with Integrated Foundation Year can opt in to specialise in the following Pathways:

  • Digital Innovation
  • Womenswear
  • Menswear
  • Textile Innovation

  • Research and concept development 
  • Ethical and sustainability agendas 
  • Creative collaboration 
  • Sketchbook and portfolio development 
  • Digital textile design: print, knit, embroidery 
  • CAD: computer-aided design 
  • Avatar digital design (including CLO3D and Marvelous Designer) 
  • Live project briefs and competitions 
  • Technical and creative pattern cutting and drape 
  • Garment prototyping, manufacturing and production 
  • Collections and product ranges 
  • Enterprise and innovation 

If you are studying the BA (Hons) Fashion Design with Integrated Foundation Year version of this course, key topics of your integrated foundation year will include:

  • Basics of fashion promotion, buying, and brand communications
  • Conceptualisation and direction in fashion design
  • Fashion illustration and life drawing techniques
  • 3D draping and garment construction on the stand
  • Techniques in fabric manipulation and digital printing
  • Fashion styling and photography
  • Brand identity management and creative leadership
  • Prototyping, atelier practices, and digital tool usage
  • Understanding industry trends and audience expectations

If you choose to add an Integrated Foundation Year on this course, you will be first introduced to the fundamental principles of fashion. Your foundation year gives you the time, space and support to explore your strengths, build confidence and prepare for the demands of project-based, industry-focused learning. Modules are as follows:

  • Module 1: Diagnostic exploration - making informed choices
  • Module 2: Technical - Technical knowledge in practice
  • Module 3: Specialist - Innovating specialist practice
  • Module 4: Integration - Integrated practice for progression to Higher Education

Please note: Students starting this course with the Integrated Foundation Year in September 2026/27 will study the 2027/28 approved modules in Years 1, 2 and 3.

Year 1

Develop core design, research, and textile skills while exploring emerging digital software, fashion ethics, sustainability and industry context.

Modules may include: 

  • Fashion Design & Realisation
  • Fashion Fundamentals
  • Fashion Inquiry
  • Fashion Design & Materialisation
  • Fashion Contexts 1
  • Fashion Identity

Year 2

Gain real-world fashion design experience through interdisciplinary projects, entrepreneurship, portfolio development and local or international industry placements/internships.

Modules may include: 

  • Industry & Collaboration
  • Fashion Contexts 2
  • Career Catalyst
  • Industry Practice Innovation
  • Industry Identity
  • Portfolio Development

Year 3

Create a self-directed capsule collection and professional portfolio for industry showcase while entering national and international fashion awards.

Modules may include: 

  • Capsule Collection
  • Fashion Contexts 3
  • Industry Catalyst
  • Major Project

A variety of learning and teaching methods are used on this course.

These include but are not limited to:

Briefings, lectures, seminars, practical workshops, studio-based project work, demonstrations, collaborative activities, group discussions, individual and group tutorials, critiques, presentations, industry guest lectures, studio visits, industry-informed briefs, field trips, gallery visits and self-directed independent study.

The course is assessed entirely by coursework.

These transport links offer an easy route to some of London's major business hubs, perfect for reaching possible placements or networking events with employers in the worlds of business or technology.  

Entry requirements

We accept a wide range of qualifications for entry onto our courses, whilst also considering the context in which they were achieved. For this course, we would usually require a GCSE Grade 4 or above in English in addition to any of the below;

  • Three A Levels at grade BCC or above
  • BTEC Extended Diploma at grade DMM
  • UAL Level 3 Extended Diploma
  • T Level (pass or above)
  • Access to Higher Education Diploma
  • International Baccalaureate at grade 25 or above
  • Other/Mixed qualifications equivalent to 104 UCAS Tariff points

At Ravensbourne we take a flexible approach to admissions, as we want you to know exactly what you need to achieve in your exams to guarantee a place on the course. We still think exams are important and look forward to hearing about your successes, but we place just as much importance on the work you will share with us as part of our interview and/or portfolio process.

Selection criteria:

Admission onto this course is also subject to successful completion of a portfolio (or showreel). 

We’ll need to see examples of your previous fashion design, textiles, new media and/or general art and design work. We understand your portfolio may still be a work in progress - please show the full breadth of your skills so we can evaluate your potential. We encourage you to include work from complementary disciplines or independent projects if they demonstrate your capabilities. 

Digital portfolios can be submitted as multipage documents or hosted via online platforms. Physical portfolios from A3 to A1 are welcome at in-person interviews, alongside sketchbooks of any size and garments. 

We’d like to see a variety of projects within your portfolio - ideally a minimum of two fashion concepts with around 8 to 10 pages each, plus additional supporting work demonstrating skills across art, design, textiles, photography and related areas. 

Examples may include: 

  • Fashion projects showing primary and secondary research, development and final outcomes 
  • Knowledge of the contemporary fashion industry: designer and brand awareness 
  • Drawing and illustration skills relating to fashion 
  • Observational drawing including life drawing and still life 
  • Photographs of design development, prototypes and toiles on mannequins 
  • Silhouette creation and pattern cutting - draping on the stand and/or flat pattern cutting 
  • Photo shoots and look shoots of finished garments styled on models 
  • Colour palette development, colour studies and colour theory 
  • Textile experimentation and manipulation (print, knit, weave, embroidery) 
  • Graphic layout and presentation techniques 
  • Product design or engineering - for example accessories, footwear or non-fashion products 
  • CAD skills for graphic layout and/or fashion illustration (such as Adobe Creative Suite, CLO3D) 
  • Machinery and equipment knowledge, including use of sewing machines and commercial construction techniques 
  • CAD and machinery skills are not essential - these will be developed further on the course using professional software and equipment.

At Ravensbourne we accept a wide range of qualifications for entry onto our courses, whilst also considering the context in which they were achieved. For this course, we would usually require a GCSE Grade 4 or above in English in addition to any of the below;

  • Two A Levels at grade DD or above
  • BTEC Extended Diploma at grade PPP
  • International Baccalaureate at grade 24 or above
  • T Level (pass or above)
  • Other/Mixed qualifications equivalent to 48 UCAS Tariff points

There are no portfolio requirements for this course.
